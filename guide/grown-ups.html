Doodlings

A toy for children who may not read yet. Draw a blob, give it a face, wake it up, and play.

What it is for

A child draws a shape, wakes it up, and it is theirs: to play with, feed, dress, give a home and show to a friend. There is nothing to read, nothing to win and nothing to buy.

It is meant to be something you can hand to a small child without watching over their shoulder. It asks for no name, no account and no money, and nothing in it leads anywhere else.

What it promises

  • It is free, and there is nothing to buy inside it.
  • No ads. No accounts. Nothing to sign in to.
  • Nothing your child makes leaves the device, unless you choose to share it yourself.
  • Once it has opened, it works without the internet. Turn on airplane mode and try.
  • The camera is used for one thing: reading a doodling's code from another screen. No picture is kept or sent.

What it keeps, and where

Doodlings are kept on the device they were made on, in the app or browser they were made in. Nothing is stored anywhere else. A different browser on the same device has a shelf of its own.

If your child gives a doodling a name, that name travels inside any code or card picture you share. A made-up name is a good idea. The home they made for it travels with it too.

Keeping the whole family

One file holds every doodling on this device, with their homes and everything they have grown. Keep a copy now and then, or use it to move everyone to another device.

Bringing a family back only adds doodlings that are missing. It never replaces or removes one.

Is this the real one?

The real Doodlings lives at bradcl.github.io/doodlings. If you see ads, or are asked to pay or sign in, it is not this one.

If something goes wrong

  • To keep a doodling safe: open its card with the camera button and save the picture. The small code on that picture brings the doodling back on any device.
  • A doodling has gone: browsers sometimes clear what a page has saved. Point Doodlings at the code on its card picture, or bring back a family file.
  • To take one home from another device: open its card there and tap the arrow that points up. On your own device, open Doodlings, tap the button beside the yellow plus that shows a phone and a code, and point it at the code.
  • A code opened and the shelf looks empty: nothing is lost. Doodlings live in the app or browser they were made in. Open that one and they are there. To receive a doodling where they live, use the button inside Doodlings and not the device's own camera.
  • Still stuck: write to me. I would be glad to help.

Who looks after it

Doodlings is looked after by Brad, a parent. It is free for any family to keep. When my kids first played it they asked, "Wait, this doesn't have ads?" That is the point of it.

It started as a small visual programming lesson I was thinking through out loud with an AI. It turned into a collaboration: the AI did the drawing and the programming, I played it again and again and said what felt off, and then my kids started asking about it. They have played it every day since, and they tell me what they think. It is out here now for whatever happens with it.

Use it freely

Doodlings is free. Share it, copy it and change it. Do not sell it, charge for anything in it, or put ads on or around it. If you change it, give yours another name and keep these same terms. Schools and community groups may use it at events, including ones that raise money, as long as the toy itself stays free and ad-free.

Everything it is made of can be read at github.com/BradCL/doodlings.
